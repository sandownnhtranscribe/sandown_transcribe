Summary of Board of Selectmen Meeting - October 19th, 2026

This summary covers key discussions regarding 2027 budgets, education policy updates, and new business items discussed at the meeting.

I. Public Comment & Minutes
*   **Public Comments:** Speakers thanked the community for attending Oktoberfest and praised local vendors and volunteers.
*   **Minutes Approval:** The minutes from October 5th (both public and non-public) were formally approved.

II. 2027 Budget Review Highlights
Multiple departments presented their proposed budgets, with motions passed for most sections:
*   **Assessing/Treasurer:** Minor payroll adjustment noted (from 4% to 5%).
*   **Tax Collector:** Proposed a 6% increase in tax program support and added $80 annual service fee for a new debit card machine. Overall gross expense was set at $93,677.
*   **Town Clerk:** Payroll costs were adjusted due to staffing changes (lower starting salary). Approved budget total: 4140-02 gross expense of $96,591.
*   **Police:** Discussed the urgent need for an officer position to fill a vacancy and cover overtime pay gaps. Motion passed approving the police budget with a net expense of $153,482.
*   **Finance/Assessing:** Finance noted a payroll increase due to percentage adjustments. The Assessing budget was tabled pending a comprehensive re-evaluation of their contract (currently up for 26 years).

III. Superintendent's Report (Timberlane Regional School District)
Superintendent Justin Krieger presented updates on Special Education, Class Sizes, and the Election Calendar:
*   **Special Ed Trends:** Highlighted that federal IDEA funding has never met its required 40% full funding benchmark. State/local trends show declining student counts under IDEA in Timberlane compared to national averages, but spending is driven more by service intensity than just student count. The overall trend indicates a significant state-level gap requiring local funding mechanisms.
*   **Class Size:** Presented data showing average class sizes for Elementary (monitoring board level), Middle School (Core model averaging 21 students; Unified Arts model averaging 24 students), and High School (averaging over 17).
*   **Election Cycle:** Reviewed the annual meeting calendar, noting key dates including:
    *   Jan 11: Mandatory statistics posting.
    *   Jan 12: Deadline for citizen petitions/bargaining agreements.
    *   Jan 14 (7 PM): Public hearing for proposed budget.
    *   Nov 3: General Election ballot question regarding limiting property tax growth for the district under the RSA.

IV. New Business & Old Business Actions
*   **Dump Truck Sale:** A motion was passed to approve the sale of a 2008 Ford F-450 dump truck to Steven Wentworth for $6,775 (the highest bid).
*   **Phone Recording System:** Motion passed to hire VOIP technicians LLC for the call recording system at a one-time cost of $18,500 and an annual service fee of $15,000. The system will be located in-house/on a server, not cloud-based.
*   **Metal Detector:** Raised concern regarding legality of metal detecting in the Town Forest and awaiting response from the Southeast Land Trust.
*   **Old Business - Detail Rate:** A motion was passed to temporarily amend the police detail rate from $93/hour to $175/hour specifically for assisting the town of Pembroke due to a labor strike at Associated Grocers of New England. This change is temporary and non-precedential.
*   **Old Business - Vehicle Donation:** Motion passed to accept the donation of a 2019 Kia Forte from the Estate of Zachary Wade.

V. Next Steps
The board needs to organize follow-up meetings and discussions, including formalizing the process for election coverage and appointing personnel to cover vacancies.
