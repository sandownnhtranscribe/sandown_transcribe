SANDOWN PLANNING BOARD MEETING SUMMARY - SEPTEMBER 15, 2026

This meeting covered standard procedural items, two detailed public hearings (NOAA Estates and Mark Caldea/Hersey Road subdivision), and extensive discussions regarding zoning regulations, particularly the "recreational fee" waiver and infrastructure standards.

I. PROCEDURAL & ADMINISTRATIVE ITEMS:
*   **Meeting Call to Order:** The meeting was called to order by John White, chair. Board members were introduced (Steve Keach, Liam Paul, Ed Mensis, Ernie Brown, Matt Devine).
*   **Continuances:** Multiple public hearings were continued at the applicant's request until the October 20th meeting (e.g., a site plan review for Bobcat Way; two hearings for Sanctuary Estates).
*   **Board Minutes Approval:** The board motioned to approve minutes from August 18, 2026.

II. PUBLIC HEARING: NOAA ESTATES DEVELOPMENT (Tax Map 2, Lot 26 / 112 Little Mill Road)
*   **Proposal Details:** NTV Builders, LLC proposed developing the property into a 21-unit open space subdivision called NOAA Estates, using a zero-lot line condominium form of ownership on ~62.75 acres. Each unit would rely on individual well and septic systems.
*   **Key Updates/Status:** Significant revisions were made to the drainage report and supporting materials after town comments and second rounds of K&A review.
*   **Outstanding Issues:** Key items requiring final sign-off are: engineering review, state subdivision approval, alteration of terrain, and Conservation Commission (CC) process to issue a Conditional Use Permit (CUP).
*   **Shoreland Protection Act Discussion:** A detailed discussion ensued regarding the need for a shoreland permit. It was determined that if no construction occurs within 250 feet of the reference line, a permit may not be required. The board reviewed the boundaries and buffer lines provided by Steve Keach (Town Engineer).
*   **Wellfield/Buffer Conflict:** Concerns were raised about individual well protective radii overlapping into the mandatory 50-foot natural woodland buffer zone. This issue requires further clarification from New Hampshire DES, as the radii (minimum of 75 feet) exceed the buffer width.

III. PUBLIC HEARING: MARK CALDEA SUBDIVISION (Tax Map 12, Lot 8 / 25 Hersey Road)
*   **Proposal Details:** The application proposes a two-lot subdivision on approximately 6.02 acres, retaining the existing dwelling and creating one new rear building lot for a future single-family unit.
*   **Status:** The applicant reported that zoning board approval, state subdivision approval, and monument setting are complete.
*   **Infrastructure/Fire Code:** The proposed driveway length required verification regarding NFPA fire department standards (turnaround area). This is considered an occupancy/building permit matter, not a subdivision approval issue.
*   **Waiver Request (Section 9.23):** The applicant requested a waiver for on-site recreational requirements and offered a voluntary contribution of $1,000 per new lot at the time of occupancy to the Town's Recreational Revolving Fund.
    *   ***Discussion Point: Fee Structure/Optics:*** A significant debate occurred over making the contribution mandatory (a fee) versus voluntary. Several board members expressed concern that framing it as a "voluntary donation" when used in conjunction with granting a waiver creates an appearance of potential bribery, suggesting alternative enforcement mechanisms or making the contribution an explicit fee would be better practice. The consensus leaned toward removing financial requirements from the waiver consideration process for now.
    *   ***Outcome:*** No vote was taken on the final approval of this application during the meeting due to ongoing discussion about the waiver/fee structure and the need for further state review.

IV. GENERAL ZONING & REGULATORY CONCERNS (Non-Hearing Discussion)
*   **Recreational Fee Debate:** The $1,000 fee per lot was heavily criticized by board members and legal counsel present as potentially problematic because it does not have clear statutory backing in the current bylaws to force acceptance as an impact fee. It should be discussed at a future workshop with legal counsel (Diane Goro) to create proper language.
*   **Road Infrastructure:** Discussion centered on whether new roads/developments should be public or private, emphasizing that establishing and maintaining local infrastructure is crucial for town growth and safety standards.

V. NEXT STEPS:
*   The planning board agreed to continue both major hearings (NOAA Estates and Caldea's subdivision) until the October 20th meeting pending state/CC feedback.
*   Future administrative discussions regarding zoning amendments, recreational fees, and procedural improvements should be handled via separate workshops or phone calls rather than interrupting public hearings.
